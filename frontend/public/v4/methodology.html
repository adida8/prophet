Methodology

How The Desk reads a market

Our goal is not to predict every result correctly. Our goal is to identify where the market price and the model disagree enough to deserve attention.

Methodology · 8 min read

In shortThe Desk compares market probability with model probability. When the gap is meaningful, we publish Pick, Pass, or Avoid. The verdict is an editorial read, not an instruction.

What we analyze

For each covered market, The Desk looks at:

  • The event being priced
  • The venue price
  • The implied probability of that price
  • The model probability
  • The gap between market and model
  • Relevant context around the event
  • Whether the gap is meaningful enough to publish a verdict

Market probability

Prediction markets express prices as probabilities.

For example, a market price of 52% means the market is roughly pricing the outcome as a 52-in-100 event.

Odds Primer translates market prices into plain-English probability terms so readers can understand what the market is saying.

Model probability

Model probability is the independent estimate produced by The Desk.

It may include structured inputs, market context, historical data, team information, and other relevant signals. The exact model may change over time as we improve the product.

Model probability is not a guarantee. It is an estimate, and it can be wrong.

The edge

The edge is the difference between the model probability and the market-implied probability.

  • Model: 56%
  • Market: 52%
  • Edge: +4 percentage points

A positive edge means the model sees the outcome as more likely than the market currently prices it. It does not mean the outcome will happen.

Verdicts

Each market receives one editorial verdict.

Pick

The Desk sees the market as underpriced. This does not mean the outcome is guaranteed. It means the model and market disagree enough for the market to deserve closer inspection.

Pass

The model and market broadly agree. There may still be an interesting story, but we do not see a meaningful pricing gap.

Avoid

The price looks unattractive. This can happen when both sides of a market appear expensive, unclear, or poorly structured from a value perspective.

Thresholds

As a baseline, small gaps are usually Pass. Larger model-market gaps may become Pick or Avoid depending on liquidity, data quality, market structure, and sport.

We generally look for a meaningful gap before publishing a Pick. Small differences between market and model are usually treated as Pass unless there is a strong reason to explain the discrepancy.

What we do not do

Odds Primer does not:

  • Take wagers
  • Hold reader funds
  • Execute trades
  • Offer personalized financial advice
  • Guarantee outcomes
  • Promise profit
  • Tell readers what to do with their money

The Desk does not know a reader’s location, finances, eligibility, or risk tolerance. A verdict is a read on the price, not a read on the reader.

Limitations

Markets move quickly. Prices can change after publication.

Prices and model reads should show a last-updated timestamp on market pages, so readers can see how fresh a read is.

Models can be wrong. Data can be incomplete. Sports outcomes are uncertain.

Readers should always check the live source market before acting on anything they read.
