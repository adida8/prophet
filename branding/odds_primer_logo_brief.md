# Odds Primer — logo system brief

**Authored 2026-05-02. The design system is locked. The wordmark and glyph are a working draft and need a fresh exploration, not a refinement of the placeholder.**

Copy the block below into Claude Design.

---

**Context: the design system is yours already.** Palette, type stack, voice, content rules, iconography — all locked. Don't redesign any of that. This brief is only the logo system.

**The placeholder we're replacing.** Inter Tight 700 wordmark reading "Odds Primer," beside a four-bar glyph (heights 6 / 10 / 14 / 18, third bar in flame). Concept is right — *charts ARE the imagery* is a system rule, so the brand mark should BE the chart, miniaturized. Execution is rough: no baseline rule under the bars (the spec asks for one), bars too thin to hold at favicon size, no real spatial relationship between glyph and wordmark, no variants drawn. **Treat the bars as one direction to explore, not the answer.**

**Job of the logo.**

1. Sit at the top of a magazine masthead next to a 48px serif headline, and not lose. Confident, quiet, ink-on-paper.
2. Read at 16px favicon and 11px footer. The mark cannot rely on detail.
3. Survive the edition lockup — `Odds Primer · Vol. 1 · World Cup 2026` — without crowding.
4. Carry no casino energy. No shields, crowns, traffic-light colour, aggressive italic, script.
5. Carry no tipster energy. No targets, reticles, flame icons, rockets, bullseyes.
6. Earn the editorial position. At home next to em-dashes, footnote daggers (†, ‡, §), interpunct separators (·), small-caps eyebrows.

**Step 1 — explore four directions. For each, deliver one wordmark sketch, one favicon-size variant, and a one-sentence rationale.**

1. **Probability bars, properly drawn.** The current concept, at editorial level. Bars on a hairline baseline rule (rule is non-negotiable). Widths and spacings tuned to hold at 16px. Profile irregular, observational — not a step-ladder. Test third-bar-in-flame against tallest-bar-in-flame.
2. **Typographic-only wordmark.** No glyph. The masthead IS the brand. Direction *The Economist* / *n+1* / *The Drift* take. Test in Source Serif 4, in tightened Inter Tight, and in a custom-cut hybrid.
3. **Footnote dagger as system glyph.** The dagger is already a brand element. Promote it. Custom-drawn †, paired with the wordmark. Typographic, not heraldic. Risk: too literary.
4. **Quiet monogram.** "OP" as a tightly-cut ligature, ink-on-paper, with a single hairline rule running through or under it. Editorial monogram, not corporate. The kind of mark a Sunday-supplement section header would use.

**Step 2 — once a direction is chosen, produce the full system. Eight artifacts.**

1. **Primary wordmark** — horizontal lockup, vector clean.
2. **Reverse wordmark** — white on `--ink`, for dark sections, social cards, video.
3. **Favicon at 16 / 24 / 32px** — hand-tuned at each size, not just downscaled.
4. **App icon** — 1024px master, square, safe-area aware. Quiet, magazine-y, not glossy.
5. **Edition lockup** — `Odds Primer · Vol. 1 · World Cup 2026` as a single composed unit. Try it inline, on a second line, and as a stamped block.
6. **Social card** — 1200×630 OG template. Wordmark, edition strip, one editorial sentence, one number. No photography.
7. **Identity sheet** — single page: palette swatches, type pairings, the chosen mark at three sizes, the edition lockup, two voice samples in their proper type, one example in use (e.g., on a comparison row).
8. **Don't-do sheet** — six failure modes annotated. Wordmark gone all-caps; the mark inside a circle; flame used for anything other than "look here"; a green "best price" tag; an emoji anywhere; the wordmark italicized.

**Logo-specific don'ts** (system don'ts you already know — these are the ones that bite logos specifically):

- Don't add a strapline beneath the wordmark unless it's the edition tag.
- Don't put the mark inside a circle, square, hexagon, shield, or any container.
- Don't render the wordmark in Title Case. Sentence case for everything except the wordmark itself, which has its own casing rules to be designed.
- Don't introduce a third colour to the lockup. Ink + flame, or ink alone, or white-on-ink. Nothing else.
- Don't design a glyph that needs a colour to read — it must work in single-colour ink.
- Don't break the hairline-rule discipline. If the chosen direction uses a rule, it's `--rule` weight, no exceptions.

**References worth studying.** *The Economist* masthead; *The Athletic* nameplate and section headers; *Coaches' Voice* (for editorial-football register); *n+1* and *The Drift* (for typographic nameplate); the Penguin Modern Classics spine system (for restraint); the 1970s *New Society* covers (for hairlines + sans masthead next to big serif headlines).

**Anti-references.** DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Pinnacle (sportsbook). Polymarket, Kalshi (right category, wrong register — these are what we're explaining, not who we want to be). Robinhood, Cash App (fintech-with-orange, too startup). ESPN, Sky Sports (broadcast TV). Anything with a flame icon, a chevron, or a target.

## Notes for next iteration

- Bars is the working draft because it's the only direction that earns "charts ARE the imagery" directly. If exploration kills it, replace it with something that earns the rule a different way — e.g., the edition lockup itself carries the data identity through `Vol. 1` numbering.
- Inter Tight 700 was chosen because Söhne wasn't licensable. If the typographic-only direction wins, costing Söhne or a custom cut becomes worth doing.
- The edition strip (`ODDS PRIMER · VOL. 1 · WORLD CUP 2026`) is doing a lot of identity work in the current mockups. Consider the edition strip as part of the lockup, not as chrome.
