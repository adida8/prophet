# Brand Decision Memo — Prophet (consumer football site)

**Prepared:** 2026-05-02
**For:** the operator (founder), Faktor (co-founder)
**Decision needed:** Which name to commit to for the football-first consumer brand, with WC 2026 as the launch moment.

---

## TL;DR

**Recommend committing to *Read the Game*.** It is the strongest brand on every dimension that matters except one: the `.com` is parked. Every other dimension — concept fit, voice scalability, audience breadth, brand-collision risk, future-proofing — points the same way.

Do not commit to *The Touchline*. The brand-collision problem is structural and disqualifying.
*The Scout* is a credible second choice but the crowded category and audience-skew risks make it materially weaker.

## The case for Read the Game

**The brand does its work in three places at once.**

1. *In the name itself.* "Reading the game" is already a phrase every football fan knows — it's what coaches praise in smart players. We are not introducing a new metaphor; we are extending one that football already loves. Compare with *The Touchline* (which exists but is cold) or *The Scout* (which works but skews tactical).

2. *In the editorial format.* Every piece of content is "a read." That gives us a content vocabulary on day one — *The Read: Brazil vs Argentina*, *Reading Mbappé's price*, *What the sharps are reading*. The format compounds and becomes recognisable.

3. *In the wedge against Oddschecker.* Oddschecker shows you data; we tell you what to make of it. "Read the game, read the odds" makes the wedge a tagline.

**Where the other two fail this test.**

*The Scout* gives you a strong format ("scout reports") but a narrow audience. The voice serves tactical previews and player markets beautifully and fights the brand in everything else (long-form columns, hot takes, human-interest pieces). The brand's structural strength is also its constraint.

*The Touchline* would be the most beautiful brand of the three if it were available — but it isn't. A football media brand of the same name with 1.5M followers already operates in our exact category. Adopting this name buys a permanent disambiguation tax: every podcast intro, every press mention, every social post has to clarify which Touchline. That's a cost we'd pay forever.

---

## The single real cost of choosing Read the Game

**The `.com` is currently held.** `readthegame.com` resolves to an "AI-powered NBA analysis" tool, parked at registrar-servers.com (Namecheap). This is the only meaningful weakness of the choice.

**Three possible responses.**

1. **Launch on `readthegame.football`** — the `.football` TLD is having a quiet renaissance with serious football brands, and the URL itself signals the vertical. Cost: $20-50/year. Cleanest, fastest path.

2. **Launch on `readthegame.io`** — modern, tech-forward feel, useful later when the B2B platform piece comes back into the picture (engineers respect `.io`). Cost: ~$30-60/year.

3. **Pursue `readthegame.com`** — likely acquirable for $X,000-$X0,000 range based on its current status (parked, light usage). Worth exploring as a Series A spend, not a pre-launch one.

**My recommendation:** Launch on `readthegame.football`. Revisit the `.com` acquisition after WC traffic gives us a real revenue signal.

## What about a hedge?

If, after looking at the mockups, *Read the Game* doesn't excite you — that is real signal worth listening to. The hedge brainstorm in `04_hedge_brainstorm.md` lists 10+ alternative football-leaning candidates. Of those, the strongest are:

- **The Boot Room** — Liverpool tactical-room heritage, rich storytelling potential, workable domain.
- **Set Piece** — modern, crisp, two-word brand with clean availability.
- **The Pundit** — direct mash of football and clarity, surprisingly clean domain situation.
- **The Counter** — sharpest editorial positioning of the hedges.

Any of those four is a credible alternative to *Read the Game*. None is clearly stronger; all are roughly equivalent.

## What I'd do this week

1. **Stress-test *Read the Game* with Faktor and one or two football-fan friends** before committing. Show them the mockup, the voice samples, the homepage hero. The right name should feel inevitable on second viewing, not just acceptable.

2. **Register `readthegame.football` and the matching social handles** as a holding move while you decide. Cheap insurance.

3. **Begin a low-effort outreach to the owner of `readthegame.com`** — friendly inquiry, no urgency, see what they want. Even if you don't acquire it pre-launch, knowing the price helps a Series A decision later.

4. **Decide by end of next week.** WC is approximately a week away. The launch landing page work needs the brand by Monday at the latest.
