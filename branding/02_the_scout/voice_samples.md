# The Scout — Voice Samples

**Voice principles.** Observational, structured, evaluative. The voice of someone with a notebook on the touchline. We file *scout reports*, we make *calls*. Headers, callouts, structured fields (Confidence, Edge, Watch List). Less prose-y than Read the Game, more documentary. The fan with the better eye, not the gambler with the better tip.

**Words we use.** Scouted, the call, watch, fix., edge, confidence, lean, fade, brief, pre-match, post-match, the report says, on the day.

**Words we avoid.** "We think," soft hedges, betting-promo language, "expert picks of the day."

## Sample 1 — Fixture preview

**SCOUT REPORT: BRA v ARG · GROUP F · FIX. 14**

**Pre-match brief.** Argentina arrives off the back of two narrow wins built on second-half adjustments. Brazil's first two fixtures have followed the same shape — front three carries, midfield holds, defence stretched in transition. The market has Brazil at −115. The market has not watched Argentina's second halves.

---

**Tactical read.**

— *Brazil press intensity:* High first half, drops sharply after 60'. Vinícius and Rodrygo lead the trigger; both have been managed through 70-minute shifts.
— *Argentina build-up:* Direct out of the back. Mac Allister is the connector; if Brazil's pivot doesn't track him, Argentina's xG climbs after the hour.
— *Set-piece edge:* Argentina, comfortably. Brazil have conceded threat from corners in both fixtures so far; Otamendi is a plus-player on every dead ball.

---

**The call.**

| Market | Price | Lean | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Argentina ML | +130 | Yes | Medium |
| Draw | +220 | Yes (smaller stake) | Medium-low |
| Over 2.5 | −135 | Pass | — |
| Argentina BTTS Y | −105 | Yes | High |

**Edge estimate:** +4.2% on Argentina ML at quoted price. Higher if line drifts to +135 by kickoff.

**Watch list.** Late team-news. If Mac Allister starts on the bench, downgrade. If Brazil rest Vinícius, downgrade Argentina ML and upgrade the Draw.

## Sample 2 — Market explainer

**SCOUT'S NOTEBOOK: WHAT −115 MEANS**

A short report on what bookmakers are telling you when they put a number next to a team.

—

**The line is a probability in disguise.**

When the book quotes Brazil at −115, the price is a probability written as American odds. Convert it: 115 / (115 + 100) = 53.5%. That's the implied probability. It's also the price *plus* the bookmaker's margin — so the real estimate is slightly lower than 53.5%.

**The line is set by the market, not the bookmaker.**

A common misread: that the bookmaker is forecasting the match. They aren't. They're posting a price they believe gets balanced action on both sides. When more money goes one way, the price moves. The number you see is the consensus of every dollar that's been bet so far, with a small house tax on top.

**Where the value lives.**

The market is good. It's not infallible. The opportunities are in three places: late information the line hasn't absorbed (injuries, lineups), structural blind spots (smaller leagues, weather, niche markets), and crowd biases (favourite teams, recent narratives, public side overload). Everything else is, for most fans, noise.

**Bottom line.** Read the line as the market's best guess. Look for the places where you have a reason to believe the guess is wrong.

## Sample 3 — Hot take

**SCOUTED: THE TOP-SCORER MARKET IS BROKEN**

A report on why the World Cup top scorer market is the worst-priced market on the board, and which two players the books have most wrong.

—

**Setup.** Top scorer is a futures market with a long horizon, low information turnover, and a heavy public-betting skew. That combination is a structural problem for the bookmakers — and an opportunity for anyone willing to read the squad sheets instead of the headlines.

**Finding 1.** Mbappé is priced like a lock. He isn't. France's path to the final is six matches; in two of them they will be heavy favourites and rest him for 30 minutes. His implied probability of finishing top scorer at quoted prices is roughly 18%. The model says closer to 11%.

**Finding 2.** Haaland is priced like a co-favourite. The squad is built around him in a way France's isn't around Mbappé — penalties, set pieces, late minutes. The implied probability says 15%. The model says closer to 22%.

**Finding 3.** The fade in this market is the third-favourite. Whichever name the public anchors on this week — and right now it's the South American who scored a hat-trick in qualifying — gets bid down to a price that has no defensive moat. By Matchday Three, that line is 30% mispriced in the wrong direction.

**The call.** Haaland top scorer at the current outright price is the cleanest play on the WC futures board. Mbappé is the hardest fade to stomach because he might still win it, and that's exactly why the price is wrong.
