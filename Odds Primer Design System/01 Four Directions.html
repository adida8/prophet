The baseline rule extends a hair past the last bar, the way an axis line does. That little bit of overhang is the whole detail: it tells you these are data, not decoration. RATIONALE

The masthead is the brand. No glyph to age, no glyph to misuse, no glyph to redraw at 16px — just letterforms that earn the position. This is the Drift / n+1 / Penguin Modern Classics move: restraint reads as confidence.

Three cuts to test. A goes all-lowercase with an interpunct (the system separator already in use), in editorial serif — quiet, modern, magazine-y. B keeps the placeholder's sans tone but tightens the cut so it stops looking like a corporate logotype. C italicises 'Primer' the way a journal italicises a kicker — the most overtly literary.

Favicon crops to the wordmark's first character — a single lowercase o sitting on a flame hairline rule that quotes the masthead. Drawn fresh at each pixel size; not downscaled.

Risk: looks under-designed at first glance. Win condition: a real custom-cut detail (the tied dd, a swash P, an inktrap on the m) that you only notice at 38px+.

Decision

Direction 01 — probability bars, properly drawn.

The bars-glyph stays. The system rule is charts are the imagery, and a brand mark that is a chart — observed, not stepped — is the most direct expression of that rule. The irregular heights (9 · 16 · 28 · 14) and the rule that overhangs the last bar are the whole detail: they tell you these are data, not decoration.

The fintech-neighbourhood risk is real, but the escape is built in: irregularity, the overhanging axis, and the flame on the tallest bar (not the third). At 38px the rule sits on the cap-line of Odds Primer and locks glyph and wordmark into one optical baseline. At 16px the shortest bar drops; three bars and the rule survive.

Step 2 builds the eight artifacts on this direction — wordmark lockups, favicon set, masthead variants, dark-surface treatment.
